01AM-GM

try the obvious thing first

If a positive-variable inequality has x/y and y/x sitting next to each other, don't invent a new branch of mathematics.

x/y + y/x ≥ 2

AM-GM. done.

Things I use when I'm stuck.

01 · basics

AM-GM

For positive x₁,…,xₙ,

(x₁+⋯+xₙ)/n ≥ ⁿ√(x₁x₂⋯xₙ).

Equality holds when x₁=⋯=xₙ.

look for

  • Products need to become sums.
  • Reciprocals occur in pairs.
  • Roots appear naturally.
  • The equality case is probably “all equal.”
x/y + y/x ≥ 2√((x/y)(y/x)) = 2.

try this first.

02 · cauchy

Cauchy-Schwarz

The standard form is

(∑aᵢ²)(∑bᵢ²) ≥ (∑aᵢbᵢ)².

In inequalities, Engel form is usually the version I want to remember:

∑(xᵢ²/yᵢ) ≥ (∑xᵢ)²/(∑yᵢ),   yᵢ > 0.

Typical moves

  • Turn a sum of fractions into one fraction.
  • Create a square of the numerator.
  • Use √(ab) or √x terms as the xᵢ.
Σ a²/(b+c) ≥ (a+b+c)²/(2a+2b+2c).
03 · convexity

Jensen

If f is convex and λᵢ ≥ 0 with ∑λᵢ=1, then

f(∑λᵢxᵢ) ≤ ∑λᵢf(xᵢ).

For equal weights,

f((x₁+⋯+xₙ)/n) ≤ (f(x₁)+⋯+f(xₙ))/n.

Common convex functions

  • x² on all real x.
  • eˣ on all real x.
  • −ln x on x>0.
  • 1/x on x>0.

Equality

If f is strictly convex and all weights are positive, equality requires x₁=⋯=xₙ.

Jensen is often just “convexity says the average behaves nicely.”

04 · convexity

Tangent line method

For a differentiable convex function f, every tangent line lies below the graph:

f(x) ≥ f(t) + f′(t)(x−t).

The useful trick is choosing t so the right side is simple.

Example

ln x ≤ x−1,   x>0.

This comes from the tangent to ln x at x=1:

ln x ≤ ln 1 + 1(x−1) = x−1.

Why it helps

If the target contains a complicated convex or concave function, replace it by a linear expression that is easy to combine with the rest of the inequality.

05 · convexity

Supporting lines

Tangents are one way of building linear bounds. In general, a convex function lies above its supporting lines and a concave function lies below them.

f(x) ≥ f(t)+f′(t)(x−t)   (convex)
f(x) ≤ f(t)+f′(t)(x−t)   (concave)

The whole game is choosing t to match the equality case you want.

06 · symmetric

uvw method

For three variables, set

u=a+b+c,   v=ab+bc+ca,   w=abc.

These are the elementary symmetric polynomials. A symmetric expression in a,b,c can often be rewritten using u,v,w.

Basic identities

a²+b²+c² = u²−2v
a³+b³+c³ = u³−3uv+3w
(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=uv−w
(a−b)²+(b−c)²+(c−a)² = 2(u²−3v)

What people mean by “uvw”

Usually the inequality is symmetric or can be reduced to a symmetric polynomial, and the expression becomes much easier to study in u,v,w.

Don't treat uvw as a magic button. It is a change of variables that exposes symmetry.

07 · uvw

Degree in w

A common reason uvw is effective is that, after fixing u and v, a symmetric polynomial may have a low degree in w.

In many olympiad-style problems, the resulting expression is at most quadratic in w. Then the problem can reduce to checking boundary cases or special configurations.

F(a,b,c)=F(u,v,w).

The important point is not the slogan “two variables are equal.” The actual reduction depends on the degree and the admissible region for (u,v,w).

08 · uvw

why b=c

In many symmetric three-variable inequality problems, fixing u and v leaves the feasible w-values in an interval. If the target is linear in w, its extremum occurs at an endpoint.

Those endpoints correspond to a repeated variable, so one often checks b=c.

That's the useful heuristic. The hypotheses still matter.

09 · algebra

sum of squares

The easiest SOS patterns are worth knowing by sight.

a²+b²−2ab=(a−b)²
a²+b²+c²−ab−bc−ca = 1/2[(a−b)²+(b−c)²+(c−a)²].
(a+b+c)²−3(ab+bc+ca) = 1/2[(a−b)²+(b−c)²+(c−a)²].

When you need a nonnegative expression, try moving everything to one side and looking for these shapes.

10 · symmetric

Schur

For a,b,c≥0 and r>0,

Σ aʳ(a−b)(a−c) ≥ 0.

The degree-1 case is

a(a−b)(a−c)+b(b−c)(b−a)+c(c−a)(c−b) ≥ 0.

One standard degree-1 rearrangement is

a(a−b)(a−c)+b(b−c)(b−a)+c(c−a)(c−b) = Σa(a−b)(a−c).

Schur is especially useful when powers of a,b,c start climbing.

11 · ordering

Rearrangement

If x₁≤⋯≤xₙ and y₁≤⋯≤yₙ, then

Σxᵢyᵢ ≥ Σxᵢy_{n+1−i}.

Same-order pairing maximizes the dot product; opposite-order pairing minimizes it.

It can be useful after sorting variables or identifying monotone sequences.

12 · ordering

Chebyshev

If x₁≤⋯≤xₙ and y₁≤⋯≤yₙ, then

(1/n)Σxᵢyᵢ ≥ [(1/n)Σxᵢ][(1/n)Σyᵢ].

It is basically a comparison between the average of products and the product of averages for similarly ordered sequences.

13 · setup

Normalization

If the inequality is homogeneous, scale the variables to simplify one condition.

a+b+c=1
abc=1

The choice depends on the degree and the expression. Normalization does not create information; it removes one redundant scale.

14 · setup

Homogenization

If different terms have different degrees, multiplying by a suitable power of a+b+c can turn the problem homogeneous.

This matters because homogeneous inequalities can then be normalized without changing the truth of the statement.

a²+b²+c² ≥ ab+bc+ca

First check whether the original problem is already homogeneous.

15 · method

Smoothing

Replace two variables by values with the same sum, often moving them closer together, and compare the target.

x+y = const.

If the expression improves whenever x and y are averaged, repeated smoothing pushes the extremum toward x=y.

This is one of the cleanest ways to understand why “set two variables equal” sometimes appears in symmetric problems.

16 · tangent

Picking the tangent point

The tangent point is usually chosen at the expected equality case.

√x ≤ (x+1)/2,   x>0.

This is the tangent/supporting-line inequality for √x at x=1.

If equality should happen at x=t instead, try writing the tangent there first and simplify.

17 · tangent

Useful tangent inequalities

ln x ≤ x−1,   x>0
eˣ ≥ 1+x,   x∈ℝ
√x ≤ (x+1)/2,   x>0
1/x ≥ 2−x,   x>0

These are all tangent-line bounds at x=1.

18 · fractions

Denominator tricks

For positive quantities, a few standard lower bounds show up a lot.

Σ a/(b+c) ≥ (a+b+c)²/[2(a+b+c)] = 1/2(a+b+c)

For the classic cyclic ratio Σa/(b+c), Cauchy gives the sharper Nesbitt bound 3/2 after the right setup.

19 · calculus-free

Bernoulli

For x>−1 and integer n≥0,

(1+x)ⁿ ≥ 1+nx.

Equality occurs for x=0, and also trivially for n=0 or n=1 where appropriate.

20 · method

Mixing variables

Another way to attack a symmetric problem is to keep a+b fixed and replace a,b by their average.

a+b=constant,   a,b → (a+b)/2,(a+b)/2.

If the target is convex in the pair, the average may improve the inequality. The sign has to be checked; don't assume it.

21 · symmetric

pqr

A common three-variable notation is

p=a+b+c,   q=ab+bc+ca,   r=abc.

This is essentially the same symmetric data as u,v,w notation. Different books use different letters.

a³+b³+c³ = p³−3pq+3r.
22 · strategy

equality first

Before trying to prove an inequality, guess where equality should occur.

Then ask:

  • Do all variables become equal?
  • Does one variable become zero in a boundary case?
  • Does a repeated-variable case appear?

This often tells you which method to try and what tangent point or normalization makes sense.

23 · strategy

spot homogeneity

An expression is homogeneous if every term has the same total degree.

a²+b²+c² ≥ ab+bc+ca

Both sides have degree 2. That means scaling (a,b,c)→(ta,tb,tc) scales both sides by t².

This is why conditions like a+b+c=1 can be imposed when the original inequality is homogeneous.

24 · warning

symmetric vs cyclic

Don't call every cyclic-looking expression symmetric.

Symmetric means the value is unchanged under every permutation of a,b,c. Cyclic only guarantees invariance under cyclic rotations.

Σcyc a²b = a²b+b²c+c²a

This is cyclic. It is not generally symmetric.

25 · useful

reciprocal substitutions

If terms like 1/a, 1/b, 1/c keep showing up, sometimes replacing a=x/y, b=y/z, c=z/x or scaling to abc=1 can expose the structure.

There is no universal substitution. Look at what the expression becomes after the change before committing to it.

26 · means

means worth knowing

AM ≥ GM ≥ HM

For positive x₁,…,xₙ,

(Σxᵢ)/n ≥ ⁿ√(∏xᵢ) ≥ n/(Σ1/xᵢ).

Equality throughout occurs when all xᵢ are equal.

27 · cauchy

Titu Andreescu's lemma

This is just Engel-form Cauchy written in the form I usually use:

Σ xᵢ²/aᵢ ≥ (Σxᵢ)²/(Σaᵢ).

The denominators must be positive.

28 · convexity

log-convexity

A positive function f is log-convex when ln f is convex.

Equivalently, weighted geometric combinations satisfy the corresponding interpolation inequality.

f(λx+(1−λ)y) ≤ f(x)^λ f(y)^(1−λ).

This shows up naturally around products and powers.

29 · one variable

one-variable reduction

Sometimes a symmetric inequality reduces to one parameter after setting b=c or using a normalization.

Then factorization, monotonicity, or a simple derivative can finish the job.

Use this after exploiting the algebraic structure, not before.

30 · algebra

common SOS pieces

(x−y)² ≥ 0
(x+y)² ≥ 4xy
x²+y²+z²−xy−yz−zx ≥ 0
x²+y² ≥ 2xy

These are basic, but a ridiculous number of proofs end here.

31 · strategy

my rough checklist

  1. Check positivity.
  2. Check homogeneity.
  3. Find the equality case.
  4. Try AM-GM / Cauchy.
  5. Try a standard substitution.
  6. For symmetric 3-variable problems, think uvw.
  7. For convex functions, think Jensen or tangent line.
  8. Move everything to one side and look for SOS.

If all of this fails, then yeah, maybe the problem is actually hard.
